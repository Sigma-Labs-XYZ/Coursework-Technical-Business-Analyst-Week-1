**Situation**

Merton Cross London Borough Council operates a well-regarded Children's Services department with a dedicated SEND team that maintains strong family trust. However, the service relies on CareTrack—a legacy case management system procured in 2011 with no technical documentation, limited vendor support, and system knowledge concentrated in a soon-to-retire administrator. Parallelly, team-level workarounds like isolated spreadsheets and Access databases have fragmented data across teams.

**Complication**

A DfE inspection revealed that the council could not produce complete timeline data to evidence compliance with statutory 20-week EHCP duties, putting it at risk of an ICO referral over data handling practices and non-compliance. While digital leadership pushed to immediately procure a replacement system to avoid a tender delay, the SEND leadership flagged that data gaps stemmed from fragmented team practices rather than technical failure alone. Furthermore, potential inaccuracies in official government data returns (MHCLG/DfE) expose the council to significant legal and reputational liabilities.

**Question**

Before committing to an expensive and potentially premature software procurement, how can Merton Cross establish a true picture of its operational process and data flow breakdowns, resolve its underlying governance issues, and achieve statutory compliance?

**Answer**

Bring in an independent business analyst (Sade Oladipo) to pause technology procurement and map actual end-to-end operational workflows, data dependencies, and partner integrations. By identifying operational root causes—such as shadow systems, missing SOPs, and manual email workarounds—the council can address data governance and process hygiene first, ensuring any eventual system procurement accurately targets actual service needs.
