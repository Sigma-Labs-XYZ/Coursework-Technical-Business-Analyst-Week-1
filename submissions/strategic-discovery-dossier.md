# Discovery Brief

## 1. Problem summary

Legacy Trust Bank's debt recovery department uses an outdated manual system which has caused many issues including missed follow-ups, duplicated activity, and inconsistent status updates. Over 50 agents currently manage more than 100,000 delinquent accounts using a 20 year old collections database, spreadsheets, and email trails. This is failing at scale because these workarounds depend on individual memory and manually cross-checking records, so effort grows with every extra account. This has directly affected recoveries, operational capacity, leadership confidence, and has contributed to an estimated 15% revenue loss. Before approving Phase 1, leadership expect a measured baseline, transparent assumptions, and hard savings kept separate from revenue uplift.

## 3. Discovery questions

Answerable primarily from data:
- How much agent time goes on re-establishing what has already happened on an account rather than progressing it?
- What proportion of follow-ups are missed or delayed, and where in the process do they fall?
- Which account types are most likely to use a self-serve portal, and what in the account data indicates that?
- How many of the 100,000 accounts are actively worked, versus dormant, or settled?

Require stakeholder input:
- How was the 15% revenue loss calculated and against what base?
- What is the fully loaded cost of an hour of agent time?
- If agent time is released, does it reduce cost or absorb more volume?

## 5. Final problem statement

No single record of account history and no enforced follow-up means agents rebuild context manually and callbacks lapse, costing handling time and missed recoveries, and the cost grows with every account added.
