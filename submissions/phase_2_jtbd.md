# JTBD and Prioritisation Template

## Step 1: Group the evidence

4 key recurring themes emerged from the stakeholder evidence:
1. Process Inefficiency & Delayed Actions - Reliance on manual workarounds, spreadsheets and repetitive admin tasks is causing duplicated effort, missed follow-ups and inconsistent status updates.

2. Poor Visibility & Delayed Actions - Poor, scattered audit trails and lack of a single source of truth is causing inconsistent status updates and prevent accurate forecasting, which in turn is causing delayed actions and missed follow-ups.

3. Cultural Resistance & System Distrust - Past failures, lack of transparency and poor visibility into the process is causing cultural resistance to change, lack of trust in the system and poor adoption of new tools.

4. Customer Friction - Customers are forced to repeat themselves, wait for updates and navigate a confusing process, which is causing frustration and dissatisfaction.

## Step 3: Write JTBD statements

- Customer: When I receive a debt recovery notice, I want to understand exactly what I owe, why, and my payment options, so that I can make an informed decision and take action (or know I can't)
- Customer: When I want to pay my debt, I want to do it on my own schedule without waiting for an agent, so that I can resolve this quickly and stop worrying about it
- Agent: When I am handling a customer's debt recovery case, I want to have complete context and access to all relevant information, so that I can provide accurate guidance and resolve the case efficiently.
- Operations manager: When a case arrives, I want to instantly know whether it's straightforward or requires specialist handling, so that I can route it efficiently and prevent simple cases from getting stuck behind complex ones.
- Finance partner: When I forecast recovery revenue for leadership, I want to trust that the activity data is complete and accurate, so I understand what's actually in the pipeline, so that I can provide realistic projections and measure improvement.
- Compliance analyst: When I need to demonstrate what happened to a case or prove we followed regulations, I want to access a complete, unified audit trail across all systems in one place, so that I can respond to audits quickly and without manual data hunting.

## Step 4: Top 3 justification

1. JTBD-04: Operations Manager:
    - Why it matters now: The current process is inefficient, with simple cases getting stuck behind complex ones, leading to delays and missed follow-ups.
    - Evidence: SN-003, SN-029, SN-039, SN-057
    - Influence on Phase 1: Portal handles straightforward cases, naturally reducing triage burden.

2. JTBD-02: Customer wants to resolve/pay independently, without waiting for an agent:
    - Why it matters now: Customers are frustrated with the current process, which requires them to wait for agents and repeat information. Links directly to the self-service transition and the Phase 1 portal business case.
    - Evidence: SN-005, SN-081, SN-099
    - Influence on Phase 1: A self-service portal will empower customers to resolve straightforward cases independently, improving satisfaction and reducing agent workload. Can give Daniel a concrete number to stress-test the ROI model.

3. JTBD-01: Customer wants to understand exactly what they owe and why:
    - Why it matters now: Highest frequency of evidence, and it explains concrete operational cost (repeat contact, agent time wasted) and customer frustration. Links directly to the self-service transition and the Phase 1 portal business case.
    - Evidence: SN-001, SN-017, SN-033
    - Influence on Phase 1: Providing clear information upfront will enable customers to make informed decisions and take action, reducing the need for agent intervention and improving overall efficiency.

## Quality check

Ask yourself:
- Does this describe a need instead of a feature?
- Would the job still exist if the screen or tool changed?
- Can I point to real evidence behind the priority?
